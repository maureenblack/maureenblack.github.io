---
title: "The Blockchain Is Transparent. That Does Not Mean Anyone Can Understand It."
description: "Public data and understandable data are different things. Cardano governance needs products that turn what is on chain into something people can actually use."
date: 2026-05-29
category: Technology
---

One of blockchain's favourite words is transparency. Transactions are public. Assets can be traced. Governance actions are recorded. Voting power is visible. The ledger exists for anyone to inspect. All of that is true, and on its own it is also not particularly useful to most people.

Information being public and information being understandable are not the same thing. I can publish a million rows of data and call it transparent. If the person who needs to use it cannot work out what matters, I have technically disclosed everything while communicating almost nothing.

This is one of the reasons products like [TapTools](https://www.taptools.io/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} matter in Cardano. TapTools is built on Cardano's on chain data and exposes market, token, NFT, wallet and raw on chain information through its product and API. The interesting part is not simply that the data exists, because the chain already had the data. The value comes from making that data navigable. A person should not need to understand transaction internals just to work out what an asset is doing, or manually reconstruct every DEX interaction to understand market activity.

This problem becomes even more important in governance. Cardano's governance system is transparent in the sense that governance actions, delegation and votes can be recorded and inspected on chain. DReps receive voting power from delegated ada, and the system distributes governance responsibility between DReps, SPOs and the Constitutional Committee.

But imagine being an ordinary ada holder trying to choose a DRep. Transparency says the data exists. Usability asks whether you can compare the people: how consistently they vote, where their rationales are, what their philosophy is, whether they have conflicts or disclosures, and whether you can compare a small DRep with a very large one without needing five different websites and several hours. That is a completely different standard.

I think blockchain products sometimes hide behind transparency because the ledger gives us a technically satisfying answer. “The information is on chain” is fine as far as it goes, but the next job is to make it useful.

This is one of the lessons Cardano should take seriously as governance becomes more mature. Decentralisation does not work particularly well if meaningful participation requires participants to become amateur data analysts. The ledger can provide the truth, but products still have to provide the understanding.
