title: "What TapTools Gets Right About Blockchain Data"
description: "A blockchain explorer shows you that something happened. A good data product helps you understand what happened. Cardano governance needs more of the second."

---

There are many ways to build a useful blockchain product. One of the most underrated is simply taking information that already exists and making it much easier to understand, and that is what I think [TapTools](https://www.taptools.io/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} gets right.

Cardano produces enormous amounts of public data. Tokens move, DEX trades happen, wallets hold complicated portfolios, NFTs trade, liquidity positions change and prices move across different markets. Technically, all of this can be reconstructed from the chain. That does not mean anyone wants to reconstruct it manually.

TapTools indexes Cardano market data for fungible and non fungible assets and also provides access to raw on chain information. Its infrastructure supports things such as market statistics, token pricing, trade history and wallet portfolio data. The reason I find that interesting is not the feature list. It is the product philosophy underneath it. A blockchain explorer can show you that something happened. A good data product helps you understand what happened, and those are different jobs.

This matters because crypto has spent years telling people that decentralised systems give them more control. More control is only useful if the person can interpret what they are controlling. If I need three terminals, a spreadsheet and a working knowledge of ledger internals to understand my own activity, technically giving me custody has not solved the entire problem.

Good interfaces compress complexity without pretending the complexity does not exist, and TapTools feels useful because it sits in that layer. It does not need to invent the underlying market, which is already happening. The work is organising it.

There is a lesson there for the rest of Cardano, especially governance. We have spent a lot of effort designing systems that make governance possible on chain. That is important, but governance also needs its own layer of products that make the system legible. DRep discovery is an obvious example. Delegators should be able to understand who represents them, what those people vote for, how active they are and what they have said about their decisions without collecting pieces from five different places. This is partly a data problem and partly a UX problem.

TapTools is a useful reminder that the raw material can already exist and the product opportunity can still be enormous. The blockchain does not need to become simpler, but the interface between the blockchain and the human does.
